# Assessment & Grading Policy — Assessment & Grades Management

This is the assessment policy used by the Assessment & Grades feature (Student 5).
It is the knowledge source for the shared RAG server's "assessment" domain,
together with the live assessment records from the assessment database-service.

## 1. Purpose and scope
This policy explains how assessments are set, submitted, marked and recorded in the Assessment & Grades system. It applies to every course that stores assessments in the system, for example ASD101, WEB201, DBS101, NET201 and SEC301.

## 2. Assessment types
Each assessment has one type: Assignment, Test, Exam, Project, Presentation or Quiz. Assignments and projects are submitted work completed over several weeks. Tests, quizzes and exams are timed and supervised. Presentations are delivered live to the teaching team.

## 3. Weighting
Every assessment has a weight, shown as a percentage of the final course result. The weights of all assessments in one course should add up to 100 percent. A course whose recorded weights add up to less than 100 percent still has assessments to be published.

## 4. Marks and percentages
Each assessment is marked out of its maximum mark. A student's percentage for an assessment is the mark divided by the maximum mark, multiplied by 100. The weighted contribution to the final result is that percentage multiplied by the assessment weight.

## 5. Grade bands
Grades use these bands based on the percentage: High Distinction (HD) is 85 to 100, Distinction (D) is 75 to 84, Credit (C) is 65 to 74, Pass (P) is 50 to 64, and Fail (N) is below 50. A grade with no mark yet is shown as ungraded or pending.

## 6. Due dates and late submission
Due dates are stored in YYYY-MM-DD format and the deadline is 11:59 pm on the due date. Late submissions lose 10 percent of the maximum mark per calendar day. Work submitted more than 7 days late receives zero unless special consideration is approved.

## 7. Special consideration and extensions
Students affected by illness, sickness, injury or misadventure can apply for special consideration within 3 working days of the due date. Supporting documentation is required. If approved, the subject coordinator may grant an extension, a replacement assessment, or adjusted marking.

## 8. Recording grades
Grades are recorded by teaching staff against a valid student ID in the format STU-XXXX issued by the Student Records service. A grade cannot be saved if the student ID does not exist in Student Records. Each grade stores the mark, the grade band, written feedback and the date recorded.

## 9. Feedback and release of results
Marked work should be returned with written feedback within 15 working days of the due date. Feedback explains how the mark was reached and what to improve.

## 10. Reviewing a mark
A student who believes a mark is incorrect can request a review within 10 working days of the result being released. The review may raise, lower or confirm the original mark.

## 11. Academic integrity
All submitted work must be the student's own unless group work is specified. Use of generative AI must be acknowledged when the assessment allows it. Suspected misconduct is referred to the academic integrity officer before a grade is finalised.

## 12. Privacy of results
Individual student marks are only shown to the student and teaching staff. Summaries such as course averages may be shared without identifying students.
